\prompt{1.\quad The 30-second skim.}
{Before reading closely, skim the proposal for 30 seconds: headings, bold text,
and first sentences only. Then, without looking back, write the research
question in your own words.}
\lines{2}

\prompt{2.\quad Requirements check.}
{Check each item the proposal meets. Anything unchecked risks ``rejection without review,'' so
tell the author now.}
\begin{itemize}[label=$\square$, leftmargin=1.6em, itemsep=0.1em, topsep=0.3em]
  \item Two pages or less, \textbf{including} references
  \item Nothing in the margins: no page numbers, name, or header
  \item A research question that is stated explicitly and easy to find
  \item Separate \textbf{Intellectual Merit} and \textbf{Broader Impacts} headings
  \item References cited in the text, not only listed at the end
  \item Every acronym defined on first use
\end{itemize}

\prompt{3.\quad Approach and feasibility.}
{Could you picture the actual work: the data, instrument, model, or
calculation? Does it fit an early graduate student with a mentor? Name
the spot where the plan got vague.}
\lines{3}

\newpage

\prompt{4.\quad Intellectual Merit and Broader Impacts.}
{Merit: is there a cited gap, and a payoff if it works? Impacts: who
benefits, how many, how would the author know it worked, and does it build
on what they have already done?}
\lines{3}

\prompt{5.\quad What is working in this proposal, and why?}
{``My reaction is X, because of Y.'' Name the specific choice the author
should keep.}
\lines{3}

\prompt{6.\quad Where did you slow down, get confused, or want more?}
{Jargon, a missing ``why,'' a paragraph whose point is buried. Name the
specific spot and suggest a direction.}
\lines{3}
